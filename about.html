About Cerebrum

A free scientific literature search engine that returns cited answers from real peer-reviewed sources.

What Cerebrum is

Cerebrum is a research tool. You ask a scientific question — how mRNA vaccines trigger an immune response, what enzymes let insects degrade plastic, whether ocean acidification affects coral calcification — and Cerebrum queries a group of open scholarly databases in parallel, then writes a summary with each claim linked to the paper it came from.

The goal is honesty and traceability. Every citation is a real DOI. If no papers are retrieved for a question, the answer says so plainly rather than inventing sources.

How it works

When you submit a question, Cerebrum queries these open, non-proprietary indexes in parallel:

  • Europe PMC (43 million articles)
  • PubMed (36 million articles)
  • OpenAlex (250 million works)
  • Crossref (150 million works)
  • Semantic Scholar (220 million papers)
  • arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv (preprints)
  • DOAJ, PLOS, Zenodo (open access)

Results are merged, scored for relevance against the question's specific terminology, and passed to a language model that writes a summary constrained by what's actually in the retrieved abstracts.

What Cerebrum is not

  • Not a substitute for reading the papers. Every summary is AI-generated. Verify anything you plan to rely on against the cited source.
  • Not a medical, legal, or financial advisor. Cerebrum is a literature search tool. Do not use it in place of a qualified professional.
  • Not tracked or monetized. No ads. No account. No analytics beyond basic uptime monitoring.
