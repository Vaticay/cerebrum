# Cerebrum: "The Observatory" — Design Overhaul Vision

## The question I asked myself
What would make a literature-search instrument feel next-generation — something
you've never seen before — across the entire lifecycle of a search?

## The core insight
Every AI search tool looks identical now: dark void, centered pill input,
chat bubbles. Cerebrum's actual differentiator is that it is a RESEARCH
INSTRUMENT — 15 real databases, real papers, self-auditing claims. The design
should make you feel like you're operating precision equipment at an
observatory, not chatting with a bot. Nobody else owns this metaphor.

## Design language: "The Observatory"
1. **Instrument precision** — hairline rules, corner ticks, mono micro-labels
   ("SPECIMEN 042 · 15 DATABASES · 2.4s"), calibrated spacing. Oscilloscope,
   not dashboard.
2. **Cinematic depth** — the footage is "the specimen under the lens," labeled
   as such, framed like a microscope viewport.
3. **Living evidence** — answers are readings, not documents. Claims carry
   visible evidence weight; citations are instruments you inspect.
4. **One continuous surface** — no airlocks between landing and app. The
   instrument is simply ON.

## Lifecycle interventions (highest leverage first)

### 1. Entry — kill the airlocks (biggest win)
Now: consent gate → landing → "Start exploring" → 5-step tour → app. Four
barriers before the first question.
Next: the landing hero IS the real composer. Type a question, it asks.
Consent becomes one elegant line under the composer. The tour becomes a
single contextual hint on the first answer. The instrument is ON from frame one.

### 2. Instrument chrome
A persistent hairline frame with corner ticks around the viewport; mono
micro-labels (session, databases online, local time). The background footage
gets a specimen label: "SPECIMEN: JELLYFISH LOCOMOTION — MARINE BIOLOGY".

### 3. The search as a measurement
Replace the spinner with an ambient detector array: 15 database nodes in an
arc, breathing gently while listening (labeled "listening", never fake
progress), lighting up as each answers — real data only, post-response.
The answer develops like a photograph: sections resolve blur-to-sharp,
the microscope-focusing metaphor.

### 4. Evidence you can touch
- Evidence rail: slim margin rail aligning citation markers to claims.
- Provenance strip: which databases contributed, as detector readouts.
- Bibliography as specimen tray: journal/year/citations as instrument readouts.

### 5. Typography as the luxury
- Landing headline at museum-wall scale.
- Answer body: drop cap on the lede, pull quotes for key findings.
- Mono micro-labels as the distinctive texture throughout.

### 6. Profiles as dossiers, inbox as the bench
- Researcher profiles: work as constellation/timeline, not CV list.
- Inbox/huddles: "sessions at the bench" — walking over to a colleague's station.

## Non-negotiables
- Honesty: no fake progress, no invented states. The instrument never lies.
- Restraint: it stays a research instrument, not a demo. Motion serves meaning.
- Accessibility: reduced-motion and contrast paths preserved throughout.
